My Thoughts
Promising as an Epistemic Practice
Iman Aghahakim, University of Tehran
Abstract: Moorean promises—statements such as "I promise to φ, but I do not know whether I will φ"—appear absurd. Beddor explains this absurdity by appealing to the Knowledge Norm of Promising. While his account captures the phenomenon, it does so at the cost of rendering many ordinary promises absurd as well, since we often lack knowledge of whether we will do what we promise. In this paper, I criticize this view and propose an alternative account of the absurdity of Moorean promises. I argue that promising itself brings about an epistemic improvement: once a promise is made, the promisor is in a better position to know that she will φ. On this basis, I explain the absurdity of Moorean promises in terms of the epistemic implications of the commitment involved in promising.
